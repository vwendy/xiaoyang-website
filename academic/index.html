Studying people as they change: methods for behavior over time

Xiao Yang, Ph.D.1

1 Independent Researcher · Quantitative Psychology & Data Science

Reviewer 2 suggests more feedback loops. Accepted.

Abstract. Human behavior is a moving target. I build methods from dynamic systems, networks, control theory, and machine learning to study how people change, and to design help that fits the individual. I apply them to depression, digital well-being, and everyday phone use.

  • digital phenotyping
  • network models
  • Boolean control
  • Screenomics
  • intensive longitudinal data

3Findings, briefly

  1. Phones are sensors. Passive phone behavior tracks depressive symptoms when within- and between-person effects are separated.[2]
  2. Emotions form networks. Feedback loops among feelings and behaviors relate to depression and anxiety.[4][8]
  3. Systems can be nudged. Boolean networks reveal the smallest push that flips a system toward a healthier state.[3][1]
  4. Screens leave a trail. Sequences of screenshots show how color, text, and sentiment predict task switching.[5][6][7]
